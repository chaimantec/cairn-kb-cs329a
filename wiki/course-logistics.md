# Course logistics

How CS329A (Autumn 2025) is run: its format, assessment, project expectations and policies. Two
sources are combined here and each claim says which it came from — what the instructors said in
[lecture 1](01-course-overview.md) (timestamps), and what the [course website](https://cs329a.stanford.edu/)
states (fetched 2026-09-13; the site notes that readings "may be updated closer to the class date").
Where the two, or two parts of the site, disagree, both are given.

## Format

The site describes the course as covering AI agents that "continuously improve themselves through
interaction with themselves and the environment": self-improvement techniques for LLMs (constitutional
AI, verifiers, scaling test-time compute, search with LLMs, train-time scaling with RL), then tool use,
code, memory and multimodal interaction, then multi-step reasoning and planning and robust evaluation,
with invited speakers on coding agents, research assistants in STEM, and robotics.

Lectures are Monday and Friday, 4:30–5:50 PM PT, in Skilling Auditorium (site). The instructors are
Aakanksha Chowdhery and Azalia Mirhoseini; the course assistants are Chelsea Zou, Shree Reddy, Kaien
Yang and Adrian Gamarra Lafuente (site). In lecture 1 the instructors say the course mixes lectures,
guest lectures from frontier AI labs — on topics from the evolution of post-training to multimodal
agents in robotics — and project presentations (≈1:00:45).

Students are expected to read each lecture's papers as the course goes (≈1:04:38). The papers are
listed per lecture on the site; see [sources](../sources.md).

## Where things happen

- **Canvas** — latest updates, lecture materials (uploaded before each class), office hours
  (≈1:01:32, ≈1:07:40).
- **Ed** — questions; the instructors ask that they be posted publicly where possible, since others
  probably have the same question (≈1:07:40–1:08:28). The site says "EdStem".
- **Gradescope** — homework and project-milestone submissions (≈1:08:28).

## Assessment

Lecture 1: three homeworks worth 50% in total, one more homework than the previous offering, and a
project worth 50% (≈1:01:32, ≈1:07:40). The site's breakdown:

| Component | Weight | Due (site) |
|---|---|---|
| Homework 1 | 15% | Oct 13 |
| Homework 2 | 15% | Oct 23 |
| Homework 3 | 20% | Nov 7 |
| Project proposal | 2.5% | Oct 10 |
| Midterm presentation + report | 10% | 5–10 minute presentations on Oct 24, Oct 27 and Oct 31 |
| Final project | 35% | Dec 10 |
| Poster | 2.5% | Dec 12 |

The homeworks sum to 50% and the project components to 50%, matching the lecture.

**The site is not internally consistent on homework dates.** Its grading section gives Homework 2 as
due Oct 23 and says the homeworks are released on Oct 3, Oct 13 and Oct 23; its schedule table says
"Homework 2 out on Oct 14 (due Oct 22)" and "Homework 3 out" on Oct 24. Check Canvas for the
authoritative date.

## The research project

- **Teams of 2 to 4** (site). In lecture 1 the instructors add that working alone is possible but
  teaming is encouraged, not least because API credits pool (≈1:03:06).
- **API credits** are provided (≈1:03:06; site).
- **Acceptable** (≈1:03:52–1:04:38): a new evaluation dataset or benchmark; a study of the reliability
  of an existing agentic system; hill-climbing an existing benchmark with a new idea; improving, or
  questioning and evaluating, a decision made in one of the course's papers. The site says projects
  should be broadly around the research areas discussed in class and benchmarks related to agent
  workflows.
- **Not acceptable**: a survey paper, or an app assembled to show something. It must be research —
  a hypothesis, a question, a targeted improvement or analysis — "something more than vibe coding"
  (≈1:04:38–1:05:23).
- **Milestones**: start early; the proposal is due in early October; by the midterm presentation,
  about two weeks later, there must be experimental progress, not just a proposal; the final report
  carries a lot of weight; the poster session ends the quarter (≈1:05:23–1:06:08).
- An independent-study overlap is fine as long as it is actual new work, not a copy of existing work
  (≈1:06:54; *the student's question is partly inaudible*).
- Students from the previous offering published papers from their projects (≈1:06:54). The **poster
  session is December 12, 4:00–6:00 PM**, with people from industry attending (≈1:06:54–1:07:40).
  Past projects are shown on the site's past-projects page.

## Policies

- **Late days** (site): 4 free late days for the quarter; up to 2 per assignment without penalty;
  usable for assignments, the project proposal and the project milestone, but **not** the final
  project report; each additional late day costs a 25% penalty; no exceptions. The instructors stress
  the no-exceptions point because the class is large (≈1:08:28).
- **Audits** are not allowed (site; ≈1:09:15). Videos will be put on YouTube eventually (≈1:09:15).
- The honor code applies (≈1:07:40).
